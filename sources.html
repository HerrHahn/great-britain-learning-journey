READING GUIDE & CREDITS

Read with the sources beside you.

The website provides learning tasks, writing support and a personal portfolio. Open the original course-book pages in the chapter sidebar. You can read, highlight and download each packet; keep the printed book beside you if preferred. Page references mean printed book pages, not the PDF viewer’s counter (which is two pages higher).

Our three core texts

  • George Orwell, Shooting an Elephant (1936): book pp. 8–15; accompanying tasks pp. 16–19. Autobiographical essay, not a conventional short story.
  • Hanif Kureishi, My Son the Fanatic (1994): book pp. 20–29; tasks pp. 30–33. Use the short story, not the film adaptation.
  • Zadie Smith, The Embassy of Cambodia (2013): book pp. 34–51. This unit follows the teacher’s seven-slide presentation; the book’s subsequent task sequence is optional support.

Classroom materials behind this course

  • Great Britain: Past and Present – A multicultural society with a colonial past, Cornelsen, supplied as “Great Britain - Past and Present groß.pdf”.
  • From “Golden Age” to Empire: completed introductory sequence. The learning journey starts directly with Orwell.
  • Q3 Short Story Material: the supplied Orwell and Kureishi slides, migration debate and final blog task inform the focus and progression. Tasks have been adapted for Year 13 advanced level.
  • The Embasy of Cambodia.pptx: the separately supplied seven-slide presentation determines the Smith sequence: author, audio and reading aloud, six guiding questions, Cambodia presentation, five expert groups. Slide 7 contains no additional text task.
  • The accompanying teacher handbook was used to check task locations and text context; publisher solution keys are not reproduced on this public site.

Practice, saving and privacy

The side entrance is an original fictional practice text created for this learning website in 2026. It is not written by Orwell, Kureishi or Smith. The Crashcourse and Advanced Test are practice resources, not the actual school exam. Word counts and practice scores are learning aids, not an official grading scheme.
